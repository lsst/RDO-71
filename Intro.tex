\section{Introduction and Background}

This document presents the Risk \& Opportunity Management Plan (ROP) that is applied to identify, evaluate, address, and manage risks and opportunities related to cost, schedule, and scope (including technical and performance) aspects for the Vera C. Rubin Observatory during the full duration of the Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST).
The Rubin Observatory ROP acknowledges the advantages of addressing uncertainty comprehensively and systematically throughout operations.
It is derived from and builds upon the NOIRLab ROP. In any case where there is an inconsistency between Rubin’s ROP and the NOIRLab ROP, the NOIRLab ROP shall take precedence.

The ROP is one of several Rubin Observatory Operations plans that, together, describe a comprehensive approach to managing risk and opportunity—an integrated framework for decision-making under conditions of uncertainty.
The Rubin Observatory Safety Policy, \citeds{RDO-15}, specifically addresses risks related to potential harm to personnel and equipment.
Additional elements of risk and safety management are formally defined in relevant documents issued by NOIRLab, the National Science Foundation (NSF), and the Department of Energy’s (DOE) SLAC National Accelerator Laboratory (SLAC), where applicable.
Furthermore, when relevant, Rubin Observatory Operations adopts risk, safety, and hazard analysis plans originally developed for the Rubin Observatory Construction Project.

The key aspects of this ROP are:
\begin{itemize}
	\item A standard methodology to identify and assess major risks and opportunities associated with Operations Work Breakdown Structure (WBS) elements and operations functions from the Operations Plan.

	\item A continuous process to review and re-assess current risks and opportunities on a quarterly or semi-annual basis and address new risks and opportunities as they emerge.
	
	\item Common techniques for assignment of budget and schedule for the anticipated response in the event of a realized risk.
	
	\item An approach and tool to measure and compare contingency levels, the remaining major risk exposure across operations.
	
	\item A single dynamic and interactive system to inform management, to support communication across operations, to facilitate and encourage regular participation of team members, and to produce standard reporting and tracking features.
\end{itemize}


\subsection{Risk Management Process Overview}

The risk management process is a continuous, proactive approach to keeping risk at an acceptable level through awareness, tracking, and response management.
\textbf{The Rubin Observatory Risk Management process is an Objective-Centric approach. Augmenting the focus on individual risk events (as done during construction), this approach aligns risk management directly with strategic goals, objectives, and the organizational vision.}
There are different types of risk associated with Rubin Observatory Operations:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Scope risk, including performance and the technical risks like the risk of not meeting survey performance requirements or deliverables;
	\item Cost Risk, consisting of the risk that the available budget will be insufficient to cover the scope of operations;
	\item Schedule Risk, consisting of the risk that the survey will fail to meet scheduled milestones; and,
	%\item a related category of risk is called Programmatic Risk, which is risk produced by events that are beyond the control of the operations management team, where Programmatic Risk can be a source of risk in any of the other three risk categories. 
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Roles and Responsibilities}

The \textbf{R}ubin \textbf{R}isk \& Opportunity \textbf{O}versight \textbf{B}oard (RROB) serves as the managing group for the Risk \& Opportunity Management Plan.
The Systems Engineering Group manages the RROB in the Rubin Summit Operations (RSO) Department.

The \textbf{Rubin Observatory Operations Director} has overall responsibility for managing and controlling operations risks.
The Director will work with senior managers to review and assess current risks quarterly.
The Director will also approve all new risks (or delegate this responsibility to another operations manager as appropriate) in coordination with the RROB.
