\section{Terminology: Risk \& Opportunity Management Process}
\label{sec:process}

This section clarifies the terminology used to describe the management of risks and opportunities at Rubin. Risks and opportunities are assigned one of the statuses in the Risk Register and follow the lifecycle outlined in Figure \ref{fig:risk-lifecycle}.

\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Candidate} ---
	Risks and opportunities in a draft stage that are not actively managed by the project.

	\item \textbf{Active} ---
	Risks and opportunities are deemed valid and actively managed by the project.

	\item \textbf{Realized} ---
	Risks and opportunities that have been realized.
	There are three available models for the trigger.
		\begin{itemize}
			\item \textbf{Specific Trigger Date} ---
			A specific calendar date by which contingency funds must either be obligated to address a risk, the risk can be retired, or an opportunity's beneficial event will occur.

			\item \textbf{Random Occurrence} ---
			Certain risks or opportunities are possible but unpredictable in timing; for example, critical staff may leave the project, weather delays could occur, or equipment may fail.
This kind of event needs an estimate of how often it happens randomly and the cost for each occurrence.

			\item \textbf{Distributed Occurrence} ---
			Identical risks or opportunities sometimes occur across different periods in the project; for example, software packages are evaluated for performance annually. This type of event distributes the potential contingency obligation profile over the designated time frame.
		\end{itemize}

	\item \textbf{Retired} ---
	Risks and opportunities that are no longer relevant or actively managed because they have already occurred or the event trigger no longer happens.

	\item \textbf{Deprecated} ---
	Risks and opportunities that were considered invalid and not actively managed.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Terminology: Risk Categories}
\label{sec:risk-categories}

The following categories and subcategories are used to define risks in the Risk Register.


\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Program Science} Category
		\begin{itemize}
			\item Astronomy and Astrophysics Community --- Priorities, needs, and expectations, along with related changes.
			\item Science Related --- The organization's science, its relevance, and impact.
		\end{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Technical} Category
		\begin{itemize}
			\item Scope --- Related to scope changes of the organization or project objectives.
			\item Requirements --- Identifying missing or poorly defined requirements.
			\item Processes --- Unclear or poorly defined technical or operational processes.
			\item Technology --- Technology readiness level and related aspects.
			\item Interfaces --- Technical interfaces, infrastructure, and the complexity of the interfaces, and related aspects.
			\item Quality --- Verification of the requirements and concept of operations to ensure performance. How well does the as-built system compare against the requirements?
		\end{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Management} Category
		\begin{itemize}
			\item Program/Project Management — Includes all aspects related to projects and programs, such as scheduling, planning, monitoring, and controlling.
			\item NOIRLab/AURA Management --- AURA, NOIRLab ratings, and other management-related topics for AURA and NOIRLab.
			\item Operations Management --- Portfolio management, finance, ITOps, safety group, and other operations-related areas.
			\item Resourcing --- Labor resourcing, shared resource availability, and conflicts among the shared resources.
			\item Communication --- Internal and external communication within the organization.
			\item Health \& Safety Environment --- Mental health of employees due to the pandemic, safety in observatories, etc.
		\end{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Commercial/Organizational} Category
		\begin{itemize}
			\item Contractual/Procurement --- All contractual and procurement-related events, liabilities, warranties, legal matters, and compliance issues.
			\item Partnerships and Joint Ventures --- Any risks related to tenants, partners, and in-kind support relationships.
			\item Subcontracts and Suppliers --- Any risk related to subcontractor and supplier issues (non-contractual), such as a supplier going bankrupt.
		\end{itemize}
	\item \textbf{External} Category
		\begin{itemize}
			\item Financial --- All sources of risks related to funding and cash flow.
			\item Legislation and Regulatory --- Lease renewals, political matters, sites \& facilities, applicable law.
			\item Exchange Rates --- Exchange rates for currencies, e.g., USD-CLP USD-EUR.
			\item Natural Environmental Factors --- Risks from weather, earthquakes, tsunamis, and other natural events.
			\item Human Environmental Factors --- Risks associated with light pollution, satellites, air pollution, and other passive human factors.
			\item External Stakeholders --- External stakeholders influencing, such as funding agencies, the public, protest groups, hackers, hostile competitors, or other active human factors.
		\end{itemize}
\end{itemize}
